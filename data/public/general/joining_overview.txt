# Joining the U.S. Military — Overview

This guide summarizes publicly available information about the enlistment process for people considering military service. Requirements vary by branch, role, and current policy.

## Basic eligibility (general)

Most applicants must be U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents (branch policies differ). Typical age range for enlistment is 17 to 35, though limits vary by branch and program. A high school diploma is generally required; a GED may be accepted with additional restrictions. Applicants must pass a background check and meet medical standards reviewed at MEPS.

## Steps to enlist

1. Research branches and career fields that match your interests and qualifications.
2. Contact an official recruiter for the branch you are considering.
3. Take the ASVAB (Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery) if you have not already.
4. Complete the MEPS process: medical exam, background review, and contract discussion.
5. Take the Oath of Enlistment and ship to basic training on your assigned date.

## Choosing a branch

Each service has a distinct mission and culture. The Army is the largest ground force. The Navy operates at sea and supports global power projection. The Air Force and Space Force focus on air, space, and cyber domains. The Marine Corps is a naval expeditionary force. The Coast Guard conducts maritime law enforcement and homeland security missions.

## Important notes

Enlistment contracts specify job training, service obligation, and benefits. Never sign without reading your contract and asking questions. Recruiters can explain options but cannot guarantee a specific duty station or job until contract terms are finalized.

## Disqualifiers (examples — not exhaustive)

Common issues that may require waivers or cause disqualification include certain medical conditions, drug use history, criminal history, and citizenship status issues. Only MEPS medical staff and service accession officials can determine eligibility.
