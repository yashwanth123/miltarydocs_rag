# MEPS — Military Entrance Processing Station

MEPS is where applicants complete medical screening, aptitude testing verification, and enlistment processing before shipping to training.

## What happens at MEPS

Applicants typically complete the ASVAB (if not already taken), a full medical examination, vision and hearing tests, urinalysis, and a review of medical history. A MEPS physician determines whether an applicant meets medical accession standards or needs additional records or waivers.

## What to bring

Bring a valid government ID, Social Security card, birth certificate or passport, high school diploma or transcripts, medical records for ongoing conditions, and recruiter-provided paperwork. Dress in comfortable clothing; you may be asked to perform basic physical movements during the medical exam.

## Timeline

MEPS can take one full day or require a return visit if additional medical consultation is needed. Some applicants receive same-day contracts; others return after waiver review. Your recruiter schedules MEPS and explains branch-specific requirements.

## Medical standards

Standards cover vision, hearing, weight, mental health history, prior surgeries, medications, and chronic conditions. Some conditions are permanently disqualifying; others may qualify for waiver review. Do not assume eligibility based on online lists — MEPS makes the official determination.

## After MEPS

If qualified, you discuss job availability, training pipelines, and enlistment contract terms with your service liaison. Upon signing, you receive a ship date for basic training or officer training as applicable.
