# U.S. Navy — Overview for Prospective Recruits

The Navy projects power from the sea, maintains global presence through aircraft carriers and submarines, and operates aviation, surface warfare, nuclear, and special warfare communities.

## Recruit training

Navy Recruit Training Command (RTC) at Great Lakes, Illinois lasts approximately 8 weeks. Sailors learn Navy core values, seamanship basics, damage control, firearms qualification, and physical readiness.

## Ratings (jobs)

Navy enlisted jobs are called ratings (e.g., IT — Information Systems Technician, HM — Hospital Corpsman, ET — Electronics Technician). Assignment depends on ASVAB line scores and fleet needs at contract time.

## Deployment culture

Sailors may serve aboard ships with periodic deployments lasting several months. Shore duty assignments alternate with sea duty in many career paths. Some ratings are predominantly shore-based.

## Nuclear and special programs

The Nuclear Field (NF) program and Navy SEAL/SWCC pipelines require additional screening, higher ASVAB scores, and extended training commitments.

## Enlistment paths

Active duty and Navy Reserve options exist. The Navy offers education benefits, credentialing programs, and certification mapping for civilian careers.

## Next steps

Meet with a Navy recruiter, verify medical eligibility at MEPS, and review available ratings before signing an enlistment contract.
