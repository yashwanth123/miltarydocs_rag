# U.S. Marine Corps — Overview for Prospective Recruits

The Marine Corps is the nation's expeditionary force-in-readiness, operating as part of the Department of the Navy with emphasis on rapid crisis response and combined-arms warfare.

## Recruit training

Marine Corps Recruit Depot (MCRD) San Diego or Parris Island conducts 13 weeks of recruit training — among the longest basic training pipelines. Every Marine is trained first as a rifleman regardless of eventual MOS.

## Military Occupational Specialty (MOS)

After recruit training, Marines attend MOS school for fields such as infantry, logistics, communications, aviation support, combat engineering, and intelligence.

## Physical standards

The Marine Corps maintains demanding physical fitness standards including the Physical Fitness Test (PFT) and Combat Fitness Test (CFT). Preparation before shipping is strongly recommended.

## Culture and expectations

Marines emphasize small-unit leadership, discipline, and expeditionary operations. Deployments and sea-based assignments with Marine Expeditionary Units are common in many career paths.

## Enlistment

Contact a Marine recruiter for moral, medical, and aptitude prescreening. Some programs such as infantry or critical skills may offer incentives during high-demand periods.

## Next steps

Complete ASVAB and MEPS, understand the total service obligation including reserve obligations where applicable, and read your contract carefully before signing.
